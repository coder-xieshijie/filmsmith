## Unreleased

- Gallery: sixteen-style README and social overview images, plus six-second animated previews extracted from the existing Handset, Exhibit, Dossier, Strip, Split and Scroll films. The gallery distinguishes historical source snapshots from current templates and limits the v0.1.0 MP4 download claim to the original ten demos.

- Six new modes for real assets (screenshots, charts, product UI, published pages, frames of real films, a product's own long page), sixteen in all; each has a card, a still, a timing API 3 template with a real-asset gate (`npm run assets`), capture or extraction tools, and moves or page changes that are pluggable adapters over the runtime's hand-offs, so a later transition set can replace a role without touching the choreography. Each moves on its own axis (owner 2026-10-21: the first versions of handset, exhibit and dossier, all lifts and flips, read as cut from one mould, and were reworked the same day): handset vertically inside a still device, exhibit by camera travel and depth on a desk, dossier by paper handling and editorial cuts with the camera locked, strip by the horizontal advance of a reel, split by one seam, scroll along one vertical sheet. `SKILL.md`, both READMEs, the mode index and choosing table, `CONTRACT.md` (form variant and cut-down rows), `transitions.md` (mode table and hand-off "Modes" lines), `system.md` (type families and scales of strip, split and scroll), the themes and `THIRD-PARTY.md` (fonts, example assets) list them.
  - **handset** (`assets/templates/handset/`, `references/modes/handset.md`): one head-on device (laptop or phone, form variant `device`) stays locked off while its screen shows only real captures; the device stays still and the screen's content moves, mostly vertically: the page flicked to its next stop (`scroll-reel`) and the next capture rising over the current one (`feed-stack`), both window-less inside the screen, pushes under the 0.75 source px zoom cap and a light that hops row to row; the device turns once a film at most (`device-flip`, enforced by `validate()`); copy in the margin, level with the region in play; the chin names the source. Real-asset gate: hash, size, people, public and private regions, zoom cap, every margin number in its region's transcription, OCR where the platform offers it, the accent ΔE00 ≥ 20 from every ringed region. Its five moves are adapters (`moves.js`: `scroll`, `feed`, `push`, `hop`, `flip`; `content.moves`). The example films the skill's own README (two captures, 2026-10-21, 28 s). Shortest cut `edges: 'cut'`, wake → feed → lockup, 12 s.
  - **exhibit** (`assets/templates/exhibit/`, `references/modes/exhibit.md`): real assets on a desk under an overhead camera, as a multiplane (index numerals 0.3, prints 1, chips 1.65; a rising camera shrinks the near plane fastest); every print shares one 16:9 mount, so the library's moves rest on the template's frame unchanged. The camera slides along the row with parallax (`parallax-slide`, the default travel), trucks (`camera-truck`) or pulls back over the whole desk and hops (`grid-hop`), and cuts on a value two prints share (`match-cut`); one pencil ring finds what a claim rests on, the ringed regions lift out as code-drawn readings at the claim only (`lift-out`, at most twice), and a gap is measured on a print's own axis while the rest of the print steps back and a chip in the way moves aside; the camera rises over the whole desk and the ring's route ends as the rule under the name. Six scene types (`open`, `travel`, `read`, `match`, `measure`, `lockup`), chips (`assets.<id>.chip`) carrying each print's claim, text entering word by word at reading pace, the moves pluggable through `moves.js`; presets lightbox (light desk, default), lightbox-pencil, grease, pencil; form variant `desk` (plain / mat); `npm run assets` checks every asset (hash, expected strings, each redrawn number where the film says, matched values in both prints, chip numbers, the zoom cap, no faces) and `npm run palettes` the pencil against every ringed region (ΔE00 ≥ 20). The example walks a frame of the phosphor demo → (grid-hop) the timing table → (lift-out) → (parallax-slide) the report → (match-cut) the loudness chart → the measured gap (36 s); shortest cut open → lockup (12 s).
  - **dossier** (`assets/templates/dossier/`, `references/modes/dossier.md`): real published pages mounted on paper on a dark mat; the rest of the page veiled, a highlighter striking the exact words or number a claim rests on (multiply over the page), a leader to an exhibit sticker with the source and capture date, the claim restated on the mat; a figure carried off its page as a paper slip (once a film) onto a ruled worksheet that computes and strikes the result; an index of exhibits; the name struck on the mat. Its page changes are its own axis, paper handling and editorial cuts with the camera locked: `sheet-shuffle`, `louvre` cut along the struck rows, `match-cut` on a value two sources print, `split-open` for the spread, the page turn and `deal`, each a named entry of `changes.js` over the shared hand-offs (a frame-level transition may also be named). Canvas2D template with six scene types (`exhibit`, `claim`, `worksheet`, `result`, `ledger`, `lockup`), motion blur on every move; presets yellow (default), magenta, coral; form variant `highlight` (band / underline); `npm run assets` checks every page (hash, size, the zoom cap, private regions, expected and drawn strings, every number on screen printed in a crop or declared in `derived`, leaders clear of text, no faces) and `npm run palettes` the highlighter against every band (ΔE00 ≥ 20, the claim legible under the stroke or the band under the line). `magenta` is a preset built from a brand colour by the steps in `system.md`, with the under-the-line rule its rendered contrast asked for; the owner kept yellow as the default. The example films the skill's own README, audio README and CHANGELOG (32 s); shortest cut exhibit → claim → lockup (12 s).
  - **strip** (`assets/templates/strip/`, `references/modes/strip.md`): one strip of film on a light box, the camera locked; real frames of real films move through a fixed gate a pull-down at a time (`strip-advance`, claw) and stop registered, intertitles ride the same strip and one lights an optical track drawn from the film's own mix; a frame punches to the full frame and shrinks into a contact sheet whose rows are exactly as long as the films (`gate-punch`); a grease pencil rings one cell, which punches to full and runs (`sheet-pick`, the climax); the strip runs out of the gate and leaves the name. Six scene types (`titles`, `frames`, `punch`, `sheet`, `pick`, `lockup`) in an order the validator enforces; the three moves are adapters in `moves.js`; presets lightbox (default), lightbox-blue, flatbed; form variant `perfs` (double / single perforation); shortest cut `edges: 'cut'`, titles → frames → lockup, 12 s. `tools/extract.mjs` takes a film's gate frame, contact row (the middle of every 4 s block), run and sound envelope from its video and prints the manifest; `npm run assets` checks hashes, row lengths, the zoom cap and the pencil (ΔE00 ≥ 20), and decodes every frame again when the videos are at hand. `score.mjs` writes the intertitles' optical tracks after the master, and every script that shows the picture runs it first. The example is the skill's own ten demo films (28 s). Fonts Hanken Grotesk and DM Mono.
  - **split** (`assets/templates/split/`, `references/modes/split.md`): one movable seam divides the frame from the first scene to the last, the input on paper on one side (a request, a list, real lines of a real file, a command, a one-line diff) and the result on the other (real frames of rendered films, renders, a captured page, a report). Every change is a move of the seam, through the runtime's `split-open` (the caret stands up into the seam, the result slides out like a drawer), `louvre` (slats out of the seam; one slat is the push), `wipe-line`, `split-branch` (two panes become three) and `diff-slider` (the climax: one changed line over two renders of one instant), each an adapter in `moves.js`. Six scene types (`request`, `pick`, `file`, `render`, `diff`, `lockup`); presets diff (paper and a deep teal seam, default), night, plum; form variant `seam` (straight / stitched: the seam and every copy as a dashed cut line, `seam.js` `colors.stitch`); `npm run assets` checks every asset (hash, size, people, a frame decoded again from its film, PSNR ≥ 30 dB), every quoted line verbatim in its file, the report against `check-film`'s own output, the zoom cap and the seam against every result region (ΔE00 ≥ 20). The example films the skill from its own files (32 s); shortest cut `edges: 'cut'`, file → render → diff (16 s).
  - **scroll** (`assets/templates/scroll/`, `references/modes/scroll.md`): one long vertical sheet joined from the product's own page (real captures at one scale, cut into strips, splices shown as gaps) and code-set quote bands; the sheet is flicked up with momentum (`scroll-reel`), brakes and locks section by section, the page's own headings pin as chapters (`section-lock`) with a slot naming the row docked under them; an underline marks the line a band repeats phrase by phrase; a ratchet docks a table's rows one a beat; one row unfolds in place on the frames of the film it describes (`unfold`); a fling back to the top (`scroll-fling`) and the paper roll (`roll`) winding the sheet up under the name. Five scene types (`open`, `quote`, `ratchet`, `unfold`, `lockup`), the page creeping while a quote is read, a speed veil and a long shutter on fast scrolls; the five moves are adapters in `moves.js` over the runtime modules; presets slate (ochre mark, default), slate-violet, linen; form variant `minimap` (on / off); `npm run assets` checks every strip and frame (hash, size, expected and forbidden strings, no faces, one scale under the zoom cap, every quoted word in its underlined lines) and `npm run palettes` the accent against every marked region (ΔE00 ≥ 20). The example films the skill's own README and the Ink demo (33.75 s at 128 bpm); shortest cut open → lockup (12 s).
