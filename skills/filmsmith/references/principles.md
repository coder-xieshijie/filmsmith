# Core principles

Every mode, template and checklist in this skill rests on these. Each principle names the pyramid layer it serves ([foundations.md](foundations.md)), the theory behind it, and the owner verdicts that confirmed it, cited by date. A mode adds its own principles on top; it never overrides these.

## Premise

Two decisions are the owner's and are not derived from theory; every principle below works inside them *(2026-10-18)*:

- **Technical videos.** The skill makes technology demos, technical reports and explainers, and launches of technical products. Its look is technical: graphic, typographic, diagrammatic.
- **No people on screen.** No real person appears: no live-action or generated presenter, actor or face.

Everything else follows the theory in [foundations.md](foundations.md). When an owner verdict and the theory disagree, the theory wins; the owner chooses between candidates the theory judges equally good, and decides when a value collides with an existing brand ([foundations.md](foundations.md#who-decides)).

## Directions

Two directions: **build bottom-up** (principles 1–3 are gates, a film that fails one is not shown), **judge top-down** (principles 8–10 decide how good a passing film is).

## Gates

1. **Readable before beautiful.** *(Layer 1)* Films are 16:9 and sized for landscape full-screen playback on a phone.
   - **Size.** Text that carries information is at least 25 px at 1080 (about 11 pt on a phone held sideways); main text aims at 40 px or more (about the 17 pt of phone body text). Smaller type is decoration that carries nothing (lettering inside a texture, a shrunken frame in a contact sheet), and each use is listed in the mode card's Type section. When larger type no longer fits, the copy is cut or the scene split; the type does not shrink back.
   - **Contrast.** Body text ≥ 4.5:1 against what is actually behind it, large type and graphics ≥ 3:1. Context text dimmed on purpose (only the region in play is lit) carries no information while dimmed and keeps ≥ 3:1.
   - **Reading, in three tiers.** *Primary* text (titles, claims, numbers, commands, questions) holds still for 0.3 s per English word, and a scene's primary lines add up in reading order, because a viewer reads one place at a time. *Glance* text (corner labels, notes, stage names, title-block fields) holds fully still for at least 1 s alongside the primary text; it qualifies only at six words or fewer, and only if it repeats something shown elsewhere or the shot reads without it, otherwise it is primary text or it is cut. *Word-at-a-time* text, slammed one word per beat, is timed per word, then the whole line holds 0.3 s per word once complete. What still cannot be read inside the scene's ceiling is cut or split (principle 5). The closing URL holds ≥ 3 s.
   - **Sound.** Delivery loudness −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP: YouTube's reference level, the default target, since platforms that normalise turn louder films down and quieter ones are left quieter. Restraint is the arrangement's job, not the level's: soft transients, no harsh highs, no sudden jumps. Short-term loudness never exceeds the integrated level by more than 5 LU, and momentary loudness rises no more than 6 LU above the median of the preceding 2 s, except at a key the plan marks as a drop (6 LU and 2 s are draft).

   *(WCAG 2.2; BBC and Netflix timed-text guides: about 0.3 s a word for primary text, a subtitle event's 5/6 s minimum for glance text; silent reading runs at about 238 words a minute (0.25 s a word), Brysbaert 2019, and the text is read only once it has landed, so 0.3 s stays the floor and is not loosened; labels are scanned, not read, Gordon 2021; EBU R 128, and R 128 s1 for short-form maximum short-term loudness; Spotify and YouTube normalisation. Verdicts 2026-10-15, 2026-10-16: the first bed was "too explosive", and the owner located the problem in volume, not effects; 2026-10-18: deliver at the YouTube level, −14; reading tiers, the size gate and landscape full-screen playback accepted with the check of the seven modes.)*

2. **Precision is a gate.** A cursor that misses its target, a number that disagrees with itself between scenes, a label that clips, a glyph that jitters: any one fails the film, however good the rest is. Every number on screen is computed by code from real data or a real algorithm run inside the project; illustrative values are labelled as such in the README. Exact words, numbers and logos are always drawn in code. *(Moran 2024: beauty forgives minor usability problems, not large ones. Verdicts 2026-10-17: a misaligned cursor sank an otherwise passable film; the computed BPE run and the 703-square data film were both accepted.)*

3. **One idea per beat, one protagonist per frame.** Pause on any frame: within three seconds a viewer can say what it is about. At most two large elements per frame, and at most three type levels in the content area (the persistent frame excluded). A level is defined so it can be measured: sizes within 15 % of each other are one level, and neighbouring levels differ by a ratio of at least 1.25. A table or diagram page may add one label level, with the reason on the mode card. The key word, number or line is made unmistakable by scale, isolation or a zoom, not by colour alone. Three levels is a practitioners' limit, not a standard, so it is checked at P1 until each mode's type scale (principle 7) is settled. *(Gestalt grouping; Gordon 2021; modular type scales, Brown 2011; Mayer 2017 signalling; WCAG 1.4.1. Verdict 2026-10-17: the code-talk film was "weak" because nothing was enlarged or pushed forward; 2026-10-18: levels defined by ratio, accepted with the check of the seven modes; 15 % is draft.)*

## Craft

4. **Transform at the turns, grow every state out of the last; keep every transition clean.** *(Layer 3)* The default way from one state to the next is a related transformation: the next state grows out of an element of the current one (a line bends into the next diagram, squares regroup into bars, a container reshapes around the next content). A related change is the cheapest change a viewer can process, so it is the ordinary means of a dense film, not a reward kept for the turns. A straight cut stays available where the next content has no relation to the current one, placed on a beat or on an action; a dissolve where time passes or the mood softens. At the moments that carry the film — a chapter turn, the claim, the closing lock-up — the transformation also changes material or meaning (a dot floods into a colour field, a word is pressed into a line), and that is where the expressive devices of the transition and hand-off libraries go.

   **The four tiers of change** (values in principle 5) separate what may be frequent from what must stay rare. *T0 micro changes* and *T1 scene changes* carry the information and run often. *T2 expressive transitions* (a device from `runtime/transitions` or `runtime/handoffs`, or a full-frame change of ground) come about once every 8–12 s, the same device at most three times a film and never twice in a row unchanged: what wears out is the repetition of one decorative effect, not change. *T3 peaks* (the hook, the claim or reveal, the lock-up) are two or three a film and are marked; a peak stands out only against a background of similar moments, so it is never one more effect among many. Each T2 and T3 lands with a sound.

   **Clean.** A clean transition runs one operation at a time, in at most two or three stages; keeps the object the eye is following on screen and the same object throughout, or else is a plain cut; transforms only along a relation the content really has (the same items regrouped, the full stop becoming the claim), and otherwise cuts or lets the old content leave before the new arrives; never shows two contents half-transparent over each other; moves on eased paths, longer for longer moves, with no overshoot in colour or opacity; and lands exactly on its final state, leaving no particles, ghosts, trails or drift. Effects such as glow, glitch or flash mark a beat or a meaning, never fill; flashes obey the flash gate in the [checklist](checklist.md). Dense and clean go together: one through-line carries the changes, each change brings one new thing, and it appears where the eye already is. A mode may be stricter: ink and thread are single takes by design.

   *(Related and unrelated cuts: related cuts need less processing capacity and are remembered better, Lang, Geiger, Strickwerda & Sumner 1993; unrelated sequences keep demanding attention after the cut, Geiger & Reeves 1993; viewers segment events at changes of situation, not at cuts, Zacks et al. 2010, Magliano & Zacks 2011. "Straight cuts are the default and devices are punctuation" (Bordwell 2007) is the grammar of feature films; motion design defaults to related transformations ("every shot is made out of the previous one", in a reference film the owner liked). Production effects habituate by their third onset while camera changes keep drawing orienting responses because they bring new information, Potter et al. 2018; distinctiveness exists only against similar items, Hunt 1995. Staged animated transitions and object constancy, Heer & Robertson 2007; congruence and apprehension, Tversky et al. 2002; motion that carries no information costs comprehension, Mayer 2017 — the cost is the unrelated information, not the change. Verdicts 2026-10-17: the three favourites transform at their key moments, the shape-morph film was "ordinary"; 2026-10-18: the owner likes very good, clean and tidy effect transitions, not effects for their own sake; 2026-10-19: films with only two or three turns felt thin, and the four tiers were approved, replacing the rule that expressive transformations sit only on the turns and number at most the chapters plus two.)*

5. **Motion is a function of time, locked to the beat, and brisk.** *(Layer 3)* Every visual value is computed from `t` alone, so any frame renders identically on its own. Movement uses critically damped springs or ease-out curves; exits are faster than entrances. A **landing** is the frame on which a move reads as arrived: a spring when it first reaches 95 % of its travel, an ease-out at 90 %, an impact at the contact frame; its sound sits on that frame (thresholds draft). After a landing the picture holds still long enough for the landing to read and for the next onset to read as a start — at least 0.2 s and at most two beats. Motion that shows time or a constant-rate process (a playhead, a scan, a progress bar, typing) may run at constant speed. **Ambient layers** are the other exception: a background drift, a slow push on a hold, a breathing glow may move at constant speed, provided they are registered as ambient on the mode card and stay slow enough not to read as an event (draft limit: ≤ 15 px/s of travel, ≤ 1.5 %/s of scale); an ambient layer is not a change and does not count as one. A landed protagonist never jitters.

   **Only the key beats are locked.** Key events (an accent, a stamp, a chapter turn, the claim, the closing lock-up, the end) sit on a beat within 2 frames, and chapter turns and the claim start on a bar line. Every other cut and landing sits on any beat: scene lengths round to a beat, not to a whole bar.

   **The pace, in four tiers** (owner-approved 2026-10-19; one beat is 0.5 s at 120 BPM, and every value in beats follows the tempo):

   | Tier | What | Limit |
   |---|---|---|
   | T0 micro change | a word or phrase entering, a highlight jumping, a count ticking, a morph step | median gap between neighbouring changes ≤ 2 beats; never more than 4 beats without one, marked rests excepted; each brings one new piece of information |
   | T1 scene change | a new composition | median 2–3 s, a single scene ≤ 6 s; at least 2 scenes in the first 5 s; scene-length coefficient of variation ≥ 0.3, in runs that speed up or slow down; a new topic enters only at a scene change |
   | T2 expressive transition | an expressive hand-off, a full-frame change of ground | one every 8–12 s (about 3 in 30 s, 5–6 in 60 s); the same device ≤ 3 times a film, never twice in a row unchanged; each ≤ 2 beats |
   | T3 peak | the hook, the claim or reveal, the lock-up | 2–3 a film; the climax at 60–85 % of the length, the scenes just before it the shortest, a release after it; the sound may drop out before the lock-up |
   | Holds | the still after a landing | ≥ 0.2 s, ≤ 2 beats; a claim being read may hold up to 2 bars, marked as a pause |

   A scene over 6 s is a rest or a climax with its reason written down; the closing card's URL hold (principle 1) is a rest. Templates on timing API 3 declare their changes, and `npm run fit` refuses what follows from the content (words per state, holds, scene length, T2 count, spacing and repeats, T3 count and climax position); `check-film` measures the render against them.

   **Reading does not loosen; the text per state shrinks.** The reading floor of principle 1 stands: primary text 0.3 s a word, flashes ≤ 3 a second. A dense film meets it by putting less text into each state: primary text entering at one moment is at most five words; a claim is at most eight, read in a marked pause; word-by-word or phrase-by-phrase entry is the default. Reading time counts while the text is readable and still: other things in the frame may change meanwhile, provided they are not new text and do not cross the text. A page that still cannot be read in time carries fewer words or splits.

   **Brisk turns.** A scene's first text is readable within two beats of its start; the next scene's first element enters while the previous one is still leaving (an overlap of a quarter to half a beat, in a part of the frame the old content has already cleared, so no text ever sits over other text), and exits take at most two thirds of the entrance's time; a chapter turn's camera or morph takes at most a beat and a half.

   **Length follows content.** No film and no scene has a fixed length. Each scene lasts what its choreography needs plus the reading time of its text (principle 1), rounded up to the beat. The film is rounded up to a whole musical phrase: the added beats are spread back over earlier scenes still under their ceiling, at most one beat each, from the end backwards, and only the remainder goes to the closing scene, which then ends with a second move landing on the downbeat of its last bar. A requested length moves the tempo inside the mode's range; what the range cannot absorb is met by cutting or adding scenes, never by padding holds or squeezing reading time. `lib/timing.js` computes all of it, and `npm run fit` writes `renders/timing.json` (scenes, key events, holds, hand-offs and the declared tier events) for `check-film` to measure the render against.

   **Why the numbers changed (2026-10-19).** Until then this principle capped a page at twelve words and four bars, let the picture hold still for a bar after each step and a bar and a half after the last, and kept expressive transformations to the turns. Four parts of that were wrong. (1) Abrams & Christ 2003 was read as a reason for stillness after a landing; it shows that the *onset* of motion captures attention, which argues for more onsets with a short settle between them, and nothing in it supports holding a bar or more. (2) "Cut by default, devices as punctuation" is the feature-film norm (Bordwell); motion design defaults to related transformations, which are the cheapest kind of change (Lang et al. 1993; Geiger & Reeves 1993), and more changes within a scene raise arousal, attention and memory without a measurable rise in load (Lang et al. 2000). The cost lies in the new information each change brings, so the rules free the rate of change and cap the information per change (Lang et al. 2013; Hendriks Vettehen & Kleemans 2019). (3) The old transition rule conflated the repetition of one decorative effect, which habituates by its third use (Potter et al. 2018), and marked peaks, which need a background of similar moments to stand out (Hunt 1995), with the rate of change itself. (4) The old numbers were calibrated on this skill's own slow first batch, not on the genre: feature films have levelled at about 4 s a shot (Cutting et al. 2011; Salt 2021), while commercials, trailers, music videos and short-form video run at about 2–2.6 s a shot (MacLachlan & Logan 1993; Bordwell 2002; Zhu et al. 2023), and the films the owner liked ran scenes of about 2–3 s with no still stretch over 1.2 s, against 3.1–6.6 s scenes and 2.3–4.7 s still stretches in the rejected batch. Platform guidance points the same way: two or more shots in the first five seconds (Google ABCD). The tiers themselves sit between these genre levels, the liked films and the cognitive evidence; scene lengths still vary and come in runs (Cutting et al. 2010; Cutting 2016).

   *(Thomas & Johnston 1981; Material 3 motion; Pearlman 2016; sustained slow motion does not capture attention, Abrams & Christ 2003, so an ambient layer keeps a hold alive without competing with the next onset. Reading time: BBC and Netflix timed-text guides; fast pacing draws attention to the execution rather than the copy, Bolls et al. 2003, so the claim keeps its reading pause. Segmenting helps retention and transfer, Rey et al. 2019: fast pacing pairs with short segments of one thing each. Length obeys the motion inside the shot, not only the metre: Eisenstein 1929. Phrases group in 2, 4 and 8 bars: Lerdahl & Jackendoff 1983. Verdicts 2026-10-15: the first cut had no tension because cards held too long; 2026-10-17: lengths must be dynamic and derived from the design principles, not fixed in the skill; 2026-10-18: the Swiss numeral's 12 px/s drift went unnoticed and is kept as ambient; key-beat locking and the spread phrase rounding accepted with the check of the seven modes; after watching the seven re-rendered modes the owner found text changes, the waits between sheets and the code reveal slow, and set brisk, direct and clean as the rule for every technical film; 2026-10-19: two or three turns a film felt thin, the theory review found the reasons above, and the four tiers were approved, replacing the page limits of 2026-10-18.)*

6. **Texture decides how good a passing film is; clean is not simple.** Among films that pass the gates, the one that moves and feels smooth, layered and finished is the better one. Brisk and clean (principle 5) never means plain: few elements, each moving fluidly enough to hold the eye and to read as technical craft. Organic material (ink, liquid, light) is drawn as a field that flows, with uneven density and edges that curl, never as a rigid shape scaling up: sub-frame motion blur on fast moves, a fixed-seed grain, highlights and shadows on objects meant to have volume, secondary motion on landings. Texture never buys back clarity: a film whose message a viewer cannot retell fails principle 3, however good it feels. Only in a reel, where the craft is the message (thread), may texture lead. A mode whose material is flat print (plakat) may leave out motion blur and secondary motion, provided its card states the departure and its reason (a departure at layers 3–4, allowed by [foundations.md](foundations.md#who-decides)). *(Surface delight over a broken base reads as cheap, Fessenden 2017; beauty forgives minor problems, not large ones, Moran 2024; emotion and story outrank rhythm, Murch 1995. Verdict 2026-10-17: the showreel had "the best texture of all" although its message was unclear — a reel; the BPE film's motion and music were praised; 2026-10-18: the print modes' departure accepted with the check of the seven modes; the owner asked for motion that is "流畅、丝滑，有足够的吸引力" and said "干净不代表简单"; the code-drawn ink bloom was rejected as "僵硬" next to the generated one; the re-rendered showreel was liked for both its motion and its music.)*

## System

7. **One system, one accent, one meaning.** Neutrals plus a single accent. Colour beyond the neutrals plays one of four roles, and only the first is the accent:
   - **Accent**: marks one kind of thing throughout the film; the mode card names which, under Accent role. As a mark it covers at most 10 % of the frame. Turning the whole frame to the accent at a structural turn is a change of ground, not a mark, and lasts at most two bars each time.
   - **Status** colours (error, warning) sit only on the item in that state and never stand in for emphasis.
   - **Category** colours in a chart or diagram follow data-visualisation practice: few, distinguishable hues, safe for colour-vision deficiency.
   - **Effect** colours (ink's misregistration) are transients, on screen for at most 0.1 s, and mark nothing.

   Two type families plus one mono at most. Each mode has one type scale, a numeric series that every size sits on (for drafting, the √2 series of ISO 3098), written on its card. A through-line object or a persistent frame keeps the viewer oriented from the first frame to the last; a persistent frame carries only real information (chapter, progress, brand, licence), never a decorative timecode or beat counter. *(Hekkert 2006; von Restorff 1933; Lidwell et al. 2003 on palette size and accent area; categorical palettes, Brewer (ColorBrewer); ISO 3098. Verdicts 2026-10-16: all three accepted launch styles keep one accent with one meaning and one persistent frame; 2026-10-18: the four colour roles, the accent area and one scale per mode accepted with the check of the seven modes.)*

## Fit and freshness

8. **No people; the picture is technical.** No real person appears, filmed or generated (premise). The picture is graphic, typographic, diagrammatic or drawn; when an object must look physical it is clean CG in the mode's palette and must hold up frame by frame; when a character is unavoidable it is drawn, anime-style. Generated near-human figures are avoided on theory too: near-human likeness lowers affinity (Mori 1970, the uncanny valley). *(Premise 2026-10-18. Verdicts 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-17, recorded with their reasons: the filmed-metaphor film did not fit the content (principle 9); the generated presenter opening was disliked as a person ("use anime instead"); the 3D teardown failed on execution (box-shaped parts, a material drop at the cut); the photoreal history film had no clear use.)*

9. **The style points at the content.** Before any text is read, the look says which world the film belongs to: terminal for command-line tools, drawing for engineering workflows, squares for counts, diagrams for mechanisms. The mode is chosen by what the content is, not by what looks impressive. Three modes are chosen by form instead of domain (ink for a teaser, plakat for a claim with steps, thread for a reel), so their look alone cannot name the domain: each of their films has at least one scene drawn from a real technical artefact of the product (a command, code tokens, real numbers), and that scene carries the signal. For them the mute test asks whether a viewer can tell it is a technical product. *(Barthes 1964; Sullivan 1896. Verdict 2026-10-16: metaphor footage replaced the product's real flow and was ruled out; 2026-10-18: the technical-artefact scene for form-chosen modes accepted with the check of the seven modes.)*

10. **Borrow grammar, not layout; write the concept before the picture.** Every film starts from `PLAN.md`, written before any scene is drawn: one concept sentence, a ban list taken from the mode card's Factory defaults and from the dated list of [AI tells](ai-tells.md), the nearest reference film, and a table of the motifs the film shares with it. The concept sentence has a fixed form, "In this film, [something only this product has] becomes [the mode's signature device]", and passes the rival test: put the nearest competing product into the sentence; if it still holds, the idea belongs to the category, not to this product, and is rewritten. `new-film` writes its skeleton; a film without one is not rendered. Side by side with its nearest reference, the film must not read as the same design. Two levels are checked. A **mode's** fixed grammar (the frame, the signature transformations, the opening and the lock-up its template draws) is checked once against the mode's reference film, on the mode card; a mode sharing more than half of the reference's motifs is reworked. A **film's** motif table counts only what the film chooses (scene types and their order, copy, palette, through-line, optional devices); sharing more than half of those with the nearest reference is a P1 failure (half is draft). When the reference film is not available, the mode card's Factory defaults stand in for it. *(MAYA, Hekkert et al. 2003. Verdict 2026-10-15: worry about being "exactly the same" as the reference. Gallery study 2026-10-16: one viral showreel prompt made dozens of films converge on one look. 2026-10-18: the required plan, factory defaults on every card and the motif count at P1 accepted with the check of the seven modes; the concept-sentence form, the rival test and the AI-tells list adopted from the TasteSkill study of 2026-10-17 on the recommendation.)*

## Process

- **Frame first.** Render the key frames as stills and fix them before animating; a still that does not work will not work moving.
- **Measure, then change.** Duration, loudness and loudness jumps, flashes, still holds, settling, hand-offs, key events on the beat with their picture and sound onsets, the rhythm of scene lengths and the four tiers of change (declared T0–T3 events, with pixel estimates beside them) are measured by script against the film's `renders/timing.json`, not judged by eye ([scripts/check-film.mjs](../scripts/check-film.mjs)). Text size, contrast, type levels, accent area and the sound's balance are estimated as INFO; check those on the frames, in the DOM and by ear.
- **The owner picks between qualified candidates.** When two values both pass the principles, make about five seconds of each, same shot and timing, only that value changed, and let the owner choose. *(Verdict 2026-10-15: "make a sample of each, 5 s is enough".)*
- **Reproducible by any coding agent.** Every film is made from a template in this skill by a coding agent that can run Node 22+ and FFmpeg, with network access while the film is set up (fonts, the pinned GSAP build, the first `npx hyperframes` run); every command and generation request is kept with the work.
- **Sound decisions wait for an ear.** A sound choice made without listening (2026-10-18: the v2 mix, the climax lift, the breath before landings, accepted on the recommendation) is marked "not yet confirmed by ear" until someone has listened to it on headphones and on a phone speaker.
