# AI tells in short technical films

Devices that make a code-drawn or generated film read as made by default, because a model reaches for them first and thousands of films now share them. A film's `PLAN.md` ban list includes all of them ([principle 10](principles.md)); the checklist asks for none on screen.

Each entry says what is done, never what to use instead: a public list of replacements becomes the next wave of tells within months (the reason TasteSkill's `tells.md` lists only the moves, studied 2026-10-17). Entries carry the month they were observed; prune one when it stops being common, add one when a device turns up in three or more unrelated films.

## Picture

| Observed | Tell | Why it fails here |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10 | A different visual style on every beat, a pastiche of eras or art movements, each held about half a second | One system, one accent (principle 7); half-second frames cannot be read (principle 1) |
| 2026-10 | A wall or grid of the product's outputs flying past as the climax | Says "a lot" and nothing in particular; no protagonist (principle 3) |
| 2026-10 | Neon gradient glows (violet to cyan, magenta to orange) on a black ground | Colour without a role; more than one accent (principle 7) |
| 2026-10 | Chrome or brushed-metal 3D lettering that spins into place | Generic material, no relation to the content (principles 6, 9) |
| 2026-10 | Particles swarming into the logo, then exploding outwards | Transformation with no relation behind it (principle 4) |
| 2026-10 | Glassmorphism cards: frosted panels with a white hairline and a drop shadow, floating over a blurred blob | Decoration that carries nothing; shadows on objects that have no volume (principle 6) |
| 2026-10 | Tiny uppercase tracked-out eyebrow labels over every heading, plus 01 / 02 / 03 numerals that count nothing | A fourth type level; numbers that are not information (principles 2, 3) |
| 2026-10 | A decorative timecode, BPM readout or beat dots in the frame | The persistent frame carries real information only (principle 7) |
| 2026-10 | One word of a headline set in a different colour, italic or gradient | Emphasis by colour alone; a second accent (principles 3, 7) |
| 2026-10 | A generated presenter or a photoreal person, or near-human 3D figures | Premise: no people on screen (principle 8) |
| 2026-10 | Photoreal generated footage standing in for the product's flow (lamps, gears, cityscapes) | The style must point at the content; metaphor replaced the flow (principle 9; 2026-10-16) |
| 2026-10 | An ink, liquid or smoke effect drawn as a rigid circle or blob that scales up | Reads as stiff; organic material is a flowing field (principle 6; 2026-10-18) |

## Motion

| Observed | Tell | Why it fails here |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10 | Everything enters with the same 0.5 s fade-and-rise from below, staggered 60–90 ms | Web-page load motion; motion that carries no information (principle 4) |
| 2026-10 | Long holds on a finished page while the music carries on | The viewer waits for the film (principle 5, brisk) |
| 2026-10 | Linear moves, or springs that overshoot and wobble on text | Linear only for constant-rate processes; text lands and holds still (principle 5) |
| 2026-10 | A camera that drifts or orbits the whole time | Sustained motion stops catching attention; ambient layers stay under their limits (principle 5) |
| 2026-10 | Flash frames and RGB-split glitches as filler between every scene | Effects mark a beat or a meaning; the flash gate applies (principle 4) |

## Sound

| Observed | Tell | Why it fails here |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10 | A whoosh under every single move | Sound on the landings that deserve one, not on every motion (checklist P2) |
| 2026-10 | An "epic" trailer bed with a sub drop and braams under a developer tool | Restraint is the arrangement's job (principle 1); the style points at the content (principle 9) |
| 2026-10 | Mastering as loud as possible, short-term loudness pinned at the top | −14 LUFS integrated, short-term ≤ integrated + 5 LU (principle 1) |

## Copy

| Observed | Tell | Why it fails here |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10 | "Introducing…", "Meet…", "The future of…", "Unleash…", "Supercharge…" | Category language; fails the rival test (principle 10) |
| 2026-10 | Three-word fragments in a row ("Fast. Simple. Powerful.") | Says nothing a competitor could not say (principle 10) |
| 2026-10 | Invented metrics ("10× faster", "99.9 %") with no source | Every number is computed from real data (principle 2) |
